## Findings and changes

1. **Inconsistent quality evidence:** V1 independently searches the position before and after each move, sometimes at different depths. Even an actual best move can have apparent loss from search instability. Critical moves now compare the best and played move within one complete, legal, equal-depth root MultiPV. Up to four disagreements between a deep played PV's next reply and the following position's shallow Best are also checked with fresh equal-depth roots; a parent PV score is never treated as a full-depth child search. If the played move is absent, one paired root search contains both moves; the old root score is never reused as that pair's baseline. Actual board/graph scores remain separate. Best requires the actual unrestricted engine best UCI, never simply a large winning score. Best at depth 12 is still a search estimate, not a proprietary classification.
2. **Excess Miss:** V1 looked at the net material at ply ten of a PV, accepted a one-pawn gain, and could call a move Miss while it remained winning or was already lost. In the supplied game Black 6...Ne4 was a one-pawn pattern; 14...Kg8 and 15...Kh8 still retained +397/+380cp in V1. White 15.Nf6+ was classified from depth 12 while already losing. The stricter model requires depth >=18 complete root/played evidence, a genuine win-chance loss and lost advantage, at least two pawns of stable material gain or a demonstrated mate, legal validation of the entire supplied PV, and survival of every immediate legal recapture at the inspection endpoint. It retains a genuine queen-winning regression. It deliberately misses some subtle/quiet/long tactics; a finite PV is not a proof of all continuations.
3. **Expensive work before any board:** V1 runs MultiPV3 on every position, deepens up to 24 endpoints, then runs up to 12 diagnostic sacrifice audits, including inferior candidates, before rendering. The diagnostic awards are disabled. V1.1 uses depth-12 MultiPV1 for base positions; publishes an interactive initial review; verifies up to twelve practical loss candidates, four non-book unsettled best moves for Great, and four demonstrated adjacent-reply inconsistencies, using depth18 MultiPV2 and paired fallback; and makes slow diagnostic Brilliant audits explicitly optional (maximum four). Depth is not reduced across the board. Great still requires the deep, full-root alternative gap and a sound engine best move. More ordinary moves have only estimated adjacent-position quality and are marked accordingly.
4. **Preserved worker/caches:** serialized offscreen worker and bounded 256-entry cache remain; telemetry distinguishes cache hits, requests, actual search/init time and failures. A per-review promise map prevents duplicate exact requests. One worker is reused. Cancellation stops further jobs and rejects late results; an already running shared job may finish. Critical failure cannot create a Great/Miss/Brilliant award, and suppresses the performance estimate.
5. **Progressive state:** the initial board remains interactive during verification. Final report updates the same board without discarding the user's custom line or selected game move. PGN identity and review cancellation remain isolated. The complete renderer runs at initial/final checkpoints, not per engine response. Fetch/render timing is captured by the browser; JSON export includes per-move and timing evidence.
6. **Book:** optional exact position/UCI lookup from 3,865 legally replayed CC0 Lichess opening lines, 8,192 indexed position/move pairs. Per-entry maximum observed source ply bounds coverage (source maximum 36 plies); no arbitrary opening-move-number labels. Source TSVs, hashes and license are committed. No network lookup or paid resource. Book is a presentation grade only for non-error moves (and this source does not cover every Chess.com book move), and does not change underlying accuracy or verification selection. Prefix attribution does not claim a full named opening has already occurred.

## Accuracy model

The win-chance logistic coefficient, move-accuracy exponential coefficients, loss thresholds and harmonic/weighted aggregation are **unchanged**. The changes concern coherent input evidence and observability. `calculateAccuracyDetails` now exposes each move's accuracy, aggregation weight, weighted mean and harmonic mean. Actual position scores supply volatility weights; same-root quality scores supply the move's loss, instead of synthesizing a false position timeline from independent quality pairs. Old callers without separate position scores retain the previous behavior. Book/Great/Miss labels alone never change accuracy; forced best moves contribute zero loss, with no fabricated difficulty bonus. Estimated Performance Rating consumes the finalized quality evidence and retains its prior, sample shrinkage, uncertainty and insufficient-data behavior.

Research reference: Lichess's public `modules/analyse/src/main/AccuracyPercent.scala` at https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/blob/master/modules/analyse/src/main/AccuracyPercent.scala (retrieved during this task). The existing coefficients and mean-of-weighted/harmonic structure are a recognizable open approach. `test/evidence/accuracy-models.json` compares arithmetic, harmonic and volatility-weighted aggregation on all six before/after reports; `research-sources.json` records the retrieved source hash. Current upstream also has a +1 uncertainty bonus and different window alignment; neither was silently copied or tuned to this game. Our model is Lichess-inspired, not identical to current Lichess or Chess.com. No numerical correction factor is justified by one aggregate reference.

Why White can remain much higher: after 3.Bxf7+ White spends much of the game in losing positions; the logistic model saturates there, so further cp losses carry smaller probability penalties. V1's worst White contributions were 3.Bxf7+ (22.96%), 9.Qf3+ (37.83%), 14.Nh5+ (38.62%), 10.Ne2 (46.91%), 13.g3 (55.75%) and 18.O-O (56.18%). Their exact weights are exported, along with first and last plies. This demonstrates the behavior of our formula, not the undisclosed reason Chess.com displays 44.0. The Black match in V1 is not evidence of general calibration. The new estimate need not move closer to either proprietary number.

## Reproduce / add another game

```sh
npm run build
npm test
npm run review -- game.pgn report.json --csv report.csv
npm run review -- game.pgn report.json --reference reference.json
npm run review -- game.pgn report.json --audits --no-book
npm run review -- game.pgn report.json --base-depth 12 --critical-depth 18
npm run benchmark
npm run book:build
```

The extension and CLI call the same `runReview` implementation. Reports include UCI/SAN, initial/final grade, scores, cp/probability loss, move accuracy and aggregation weight, depths, verification source, best PV, alternative loss, Miss/Brilliant evidence, performance estimates, timings and transport metrics. `test/evidence/screenshot-reference.json` demonstrates aggregate reference format. A per-move reference must supply a matching PGN SHA256 or starting FEN plus every UCI and grade; then the harness produces agreement, a confusion matrix, false Miss counts and mistake/blunder disagreements. Aggregate-only input cannot produce those metrics. No paid review data is scraped.

Cold benchmarks create fresh workers/caches per case and run sequentially. The 20-ply case is an explicitly unfinished prefix of the supplied PGN; 42 is its complete game; 80 is an explicitly unfinished prefix of the sourced Fischer–Spassky game 6. They are legal measurement inputs, not fabricated completed games. Raw before/after reports and environment metadata are committed. Before/after UI/fetch timing is **not** measured by a Node CLI: it uses local PGNs and no DOM. Live browser instrumentation supplies those fields when the extension runs. Node IPC stands in for browser Worker IPC around the actual same WASM and offscreen source.

Exact CPU/memory metadata is in benchmarks.json. Each version/case used one initialized worker without concurrent benchmark engines. Reports show exact request counts and average request time. A repeat cold run of the supplied game took 66.72 seconds with identical labels/accuracy, illustrating wall-time variability. Reusing that worker/cache for the same game took **0.557 seconds**, **zero new searches**, **72/72 transport cache hits**, and identical grades/accuracy (`warm-cache.json`). Low cold-cache hits are expected because duplicate/deep work is avoided rather than deliberately requested to inflate hit rate.

Practical soft budgets on this benchmark machine: initial review ≤3/4/8 seconds for these 20/42/80 inputs; final default review ≤60/100/75 seconds respectively. These are observed engineering budgets, not flaky unit-test time limits or promises across hardware. Explicit Brilliant diagnostics are outside the default budget. PGN fetch and DOM render are not measured by this CLI benchmark; browser telemetry records them, and live-extension measurement remains pending. Baseline phases group all work after base analysis (deep verification, diagnostics and grading); do not misrepresent that combined time as a separately timed Brilliant phase. V1.1 records them separately. A finite-depth rank can still change without an observable PV conflict, so the bounded reply check is not a guarantee that every shallow Best is confirmed at depth 18. Miss timing is a subset of classification time, not an additive extra phase.

## Supplied-game comparison after optimization

Final recorded estimates are **White 68.3447%, Black 68.8414%**, versus the supplied 44.0/66.8 proprietary aggregate reference. The White discrepancy increased rather than decreased; there is no claim that the new formula matches Chess.com. Numerical coefficients were not changed to hide this result. Root consistency, conservative tactical labels and speed improve independently of proprietary agreement.

The supplied game now has White: Book 2, Great 1, Best 5, Excellent 1, Good 4, Inaccuracy 2, Mistake 4, Blunder 2, Miss 0. Black: Book 1, Great 2, Best 8, Excellent 1, Good 4, Inaccuracy 0, Mistake 3, Blunder 2, Miss 0. Both totals are 21; automatic Brilliant is zero. These are computed labels, not invented reference labels. Black Miss labels fall from three to zero, but precision/recall against Chess.com cannot be computed without a matching per-move reference. The extra reply consistency checks changed 14...Kg8 and 16.Qd3 from shallow Best to depth-18 Good, without named-move rules. Best still differs because actual engine preferred moves and search policy differ; no arbitrary cap was imposed.

Across the three benchmark inputs, grading changes and before/after accuracy are recorded per ply in benchmarks.json. Some changes are Book presentation, some remove incoherent horizon losses, and others replace unsupported Miss labels. Not every high-impact V1 label stays unchanged: each recorded change has before/after probability loss and legal move identity for review. The same cold-search evidence is deterministic, while changing MultiPV/hash history can change the engine's finite-depth output. This is a disclosed quality/search trade-off, not a guarantee of grade stability under every depth.

## Remaining limitations / release decision

Automatic Brilliant awards remain **disabled**. Existing Marshall, Catalan, Rxd7 and historical/negative fixtures remain release gates; no sacrifice eligibility thresholds are loosened. Diagnostic eligibility is not a Chess.com label. The four-audit UI budget and conservative prechecks can omit candidates; direct harness/calibration audits remain available. Miss intentionally favors precision and can miss a genuine tactic that gains only a pawn, stays winning, is initially losing, is quiet/long or lacks deep evidence. Great verification is bounded and can miss a later unique quiet move.

The earlier Qb6 example is not present anywhere in this checkout or the supplied PGN. No substitute Qb6 position or invented outcome was created. The exact position is needed to validate that named example; other genuine and negative Miss regressions are provided now.

The new results are not claimed closer to Chess.com across a population. There is no independent labeled calibration dataset. Public release still requires real Windows Chrome/Chess.com acceptance. Unpacked-extension loading is attempted legitimately by the browser tests; administrator policy must not be bypassed. Existing fixtures test the actual components, and the standalone browser Worker uses actual WASM, but cannot establish live endpoint/installed-extension compatibility.
