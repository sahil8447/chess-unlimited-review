## Confirmed defects and fixes

**Offer causality.** The frozen V1.1 audit actually marks `4...g6` eligible. The same c5 pawn was already capturable by Qxc5 for a comparable 100cp exposure before the move. The old detector computed material cost after a capture but did not require the move to cause that offer. The new detector rejects it before any engine search: `isSacrifice: false`, `eligible: false`, attributable material cost zero, separate exposure cost 100cp.

The before-position comparison tests legal, king-safe captures of the same unmoved unit, including pins, then compares bounded legal exchange costs. Its temporary side-to-move change is a **static threat comparison, not an invented game pass or claimed historical continuation**. Newly legal captures and increased investment can establish causality. An existing unrelated threat cannot. Unsupported/budget-limited evidence fails closed.

A narrow, structural maintained-gambit case preserves Catalan: a voluntary pawn move opens a previously unavailable home-bishop development route while declining an available central pawn exchange. It requires actual material investment and all existing engine quality/compensation gates. Check evasions and arbitrary quiet moves do not qualify. Tests include an unrelated hanging rook, an unpinning offer and a color-mirrored Catalan where Black's g6 is genuinely supported; no named-move exception exists. Marshall, Rxd7 and historical sacrifices remain regression gates. Automatic Brilliant awards remain **disabled**. This conservative relation can miss other strategic maintained offers and is not a general proof of human intent.

**Mate normalization.** New game-review records distinguish centipawns, mate-in-N, checkmate outcome, draw and unknown results. Mates have nullable CP fields and `cpLoss: null`. Mate probabilities and mate ordering are explicit; Great alternatives do not convert mates into giant CP scores. Board, graph, live analysis, detail table and explanations display mate states rather than ±100-pawn evaluations. The older tactical-audit compatibility API retains internal numeric ordering proxies; those are not the new game move-loss/display representation.

Actual depth-18 probes for 20.Kh3 found a finite losing best alternative (-1464cp in that independent search), while the played line was mate against White in two. Depth 20 still returned a finite best alternative (-1632cp). The full review's different warm-hash/search context prefers Qc3 and scores its best line -1311cp; it independently confirms the played mate-in-two. These finite-depth results show a way to avoid the **known searched mate**, not proof White can save the game indefinitely.

Therefore 20.Kh3 becomes **Mistake**, because it concedes a verified mate from a finite losing evaluation; it is not automatically Blunder from a fictitious 8997cp loss. 20...Qh6+ retains the forced mate and is Best. After it, both legal White king moves lose to mate in one: 21.Kg4 is Good with an already-forced-loss explanation, not a new lost-game Blunder. 21...Nxh2# is Best and explicitly delivers checkmate. Tests also cover a defensible-to-mate Blunder, best/forced defense, shortening an existing mate, giving up a forced win, unknown and contradictory scores.

Quality evaluations refer to the **before-position root** (best line versus played line at equal depth). Position evaluations refer to the **actual displayed board**, with White's perspective. Thus Qh6+'s root line can be +M2 while the board after that move is -M1 from White's perspective; those are different origins, not inconsistent signs. `scorePerspectives`, typed fields and root-line evidence make this explicit.

**Snapshots and lifecycle.** V1.1's initial and pre-diagnostic renderers held references to metrics and rows that the engine continued mutating. `totalMs` was assigned only at the very end, while the renderer's download closure fixed `complete: false`. A 42-record snapshot could therefore legitimately have diagnostics still running, yet mix later audit/engine data with a zero total. The quoted export is consistent with that mechanism, but its actual capture/cancellation point cannot be confirmed without the missing file.

Schema-2 snapshots deep-copy the report and record elapsed time at each publication. States distinguish `initial-ready`, `critical-running`, `diagnostics-running`, `complete`, `partial`, `canceled` and `failed`. `complete` means **all requested, budgeted analysis stages finished without recorded errors**, not merely that all game moves were imported. `fullGameImported` means a completed result is declared by the supplied PGN and its full supplied mainline was imported; it is not independent server verification of a resignation PGN's completeness. Critical completion is retained when optional diagnostics are canceled. Preview downloads remain explicitly non-final and cannot acquire later mutable metrics. Final downloads use the authoritative snapshot, not a renderer-supplied boolean.

Timing definitions travel with exports. `totalMs` is wall time to the snapshot, excluding PGN fetch and final rendering. Request time includes IPC/cache/initialization waits and is nested in base/critical/audit phases. Engine time is non-cached successful search time nested in request time. Miss is part of classification; progressive assembly/render can also be nested in critical wall time. Render/fetch fields are not blindly added to overlapping totals. Failed or uninstrumented requests are counted as unknown rather than fabricated successful searches.

## Verification policy and profiling

Default base depth remains 12. Ordinary critical moves first get an unrestricted depth-18 MultiPV1 root; if needed, a depth-18 paired search compares its best move with the played move. This saves work versus searching two unrestricted lines and then repeating a pair that omits the played move. Neither the old root score nor a parent PV score is mixed into the pair. Great candidates still require full alternative evidence. Mate-state transitions receive a separate bounded check even when the probability-loss model is saturated. Four bounded adjacent-reply conflicts remain supported. Forced moves/obvious recaptures cannot receive Great just from a raw swing.

The standard budgets are 12 practical-loss, 4 Great, 4 mate-transition and 4 reply-consistency candidates; optional Brilliant diagnostics have a separate four-candidate budget and causal prechecks. The UI now updates the existing interactive board periodically as deep checks finish. More detailed verification is explicitly available in the panel and `--detailed` CLI option; it doubles the practical-loss budget and uses fuller unrestricted roots. It does not enable automatic Brilliant awards.

`before-verification-plan.json` records an actual frozen V1.1 replay: 18 verified moves, 42 base searches, 30 deep searches, one initialized worker. Reasons and request counts are recorded per ply. `after-42.json` records the new plan: 19 selected moves, including 20.Kh3's mate-transition check, 75 searches total. Every request records position, depth, MultiPV/search pool, phase, cache status and timing. The missing browser's quoted 79 requests cannot be reconciled exactly; optional audit work is a plausible additional stage, not a proven explanation of those seven requests.

## Accuracy investigation

The logistic coefficient, move-accuracy exponential and weighted/harmonic aggregation constants are unchanged. All 21 moves per color are included. Book presentation does not change accuracy. Forced/mated positions, result, unknown scores and separate quality/position perspectives are explicit. Performance Rating consumes typed evidence and gives zero weight to already forced-mate positions; its prior, bounds and insufficient-data behavior remain.

The new reproduction gives **68.4232404076921% White / 67.62576348609826% Black**. That does not reproduce Chess.com's 44.0 / 66.8, and no claim of empirical calibration is made. The probability model discounts further errors in already badly losing positions; this explains high late White contributions under our own formula. The qualitative mate-concession grade can therefore be Mistake while its probability-loss accuracy penalty is small. Grade and accuracy are documented different heuristics.

`accuracy-analysis.json` compares arithmetic and harmonic aggregation and a clearly labeled nontrivial-position subset across all six before/after 20/42/80 reports. It also projects the same new evidence through the old sentinel-based probability calculation, purely as a diagnostic. That projection changes the aggregate only at floating-point precision: fixing sentinel representation is essential for semantics and explanations, but is not a numerical solution to the 24-point White discrepancy. Changes in new aggregate values principally reflect different consistent finite-depth engine evidence. There is no labeled multi-game reference corpus to justify selecting a new accuracy formula or penalizing all losing moves uniformly.
